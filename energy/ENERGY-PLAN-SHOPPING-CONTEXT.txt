10. TRAPS TO CHECK ON EVERY OFFER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. FIT DAILY CAPS. Many high-headline FIT offers cap credited export (e.g.
    "10.5c/kWh for the first 10 kWh/day, 0c after"). His export averages
    28.7 kWh/day and hits 81.8 kWh/day max. A 10 kWh/day cap only credits
    ~3,650 of 10,554 kWh. ALWAYS model the cap against actual daily export,
    not the annual total. A capped 10c offer can be worse than an uncapped 5c.

 2. FIT RATE TIERS / TIME-VARYING FIT. Some plans pay near-zero midday (when
    ~85% of his export happens, 10am–4pm) and more in the evening (when he
    exports almost nothing). Such a plan is worth ~$0 to him. Check the FIT
    time bands against the export column in section 5.

 3. DEMAND TARIFFS. Powercor offers demand-based network tariffs. Given the
    sharp 5–7am spike (up to ~12+ kW with the 22kW charger running), a demand
    charge could be expensive. Avoid demand tariffs unless explicitly modelled,
    OR note that the battery could shave the peak if charging is shifted.

 4. "SOLAR SOAK" / MIDDAY-DISCOUNT BANDS ARE WORTH ~NOTHING HERE. Plans that
    discount 11am–4pm look attractive but he only imports ~544 kWh/yr in that
    window (4.6%) because his own solar covers it. His current v3 "Smart Rate"
    band is exactly this and delivers $0. Do not credit these plans for it.

 5. CONDITIONAL / PAY-ON-TIME DISCOUNTS and benefit-period expiry — check what
    the rate reverts to after 12 months.

 6. EXIT FEES on the current Lumo plan, and the fact that current rates are
    FIXED UNTIL 30 SEP 2026 (so a switch before then gives up a locked rate).

 7. SINGLE vs 3-PHASE and the 5 kW export limit — confirm no offer requires
    changes there.

12. DATA PROVENANCE & CAVEATS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 - Import/export figures: Powercor myEnergy 30-min interval CSV (NEM12-derived),
   loaded into a local database. Verified to match Lumo invoices to within
   whole-kWh rounding (retailer rounds each band before pricing; a few cents).
 - Costs in this document are computed at the CURRENT v3 tariff on measured
   volumes ("what would this usage cost today"), NOT the historical amounts
   billed at the time. That makes plans comparable on usage alone.
 - The 12-month window used is 18 Aug 2025 – 17 Aug 2026.
 - Tesla charging figures blend a Tessie session-history export (to 10 Jul 2026)
   and live Tesla Fleet API samples (from 1 Aug 2026). There is a gap
   11–31 Jul 2026 with no car data, so car-specific numbers are approximate
   (±, and the solar-vs-grid split for the car is an UPPER BOUND on grid use:
   per 30-min interval the car's grid draw is capped at whole-home grid import,
   and other loads compete for that same import).
 - Import/export/total-cost figures do NOT depend on the Tesla data and are exact.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
G6. TRAPS ON GAS OFFERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 - Gas has NO interval data. The meter is read ~2-monthly and the invoice states
   consumption is "apportioned evenly over number of days". Every daily figure
   above is DERIVED. Do not attempt time-of-day gas analysis.
 - Compare on BOTH the supply charge and the winter rate. A headline low summer
   rate is close to worthless here.
 - Watch for stepped/block tariffs: his winter periods are large (3,799 MJ), so
   a plan whose step 1 is small could push volume into a worse block.
 - Confirm the season definitions (this plan: Summer 1 Oct–31 May). Retailers
   define these differently, which shifts which rate applies to his big winter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
G7. ELECTRIFICATION — the biggest lever, flagged for future analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Because 87% of gas is space heating and 46% of the bill is a fixed charge,
replacing gas heating with a reverse-cycle heat pump and DISCONNECTING gas is
worth far more than any gas plan switch:

   Heat to replace ..... ~11,571 MJ/yr = ~3,214 kWh thermal
   Heat pump @ COP 4 ... ~804 kWh/yr electricity = ~$150/yr at off-peak 18.678c
   Gas cost today ...... ~$410/yr usage + $369/yr supply = ~$806/yr
   INDICATIVE SAVING ... ~$650/yr, most of it from killing the supply charge

   CAVEATS to model properly before acting:
     - Adds ~800 kWh to WINTER electricity import, exactly when his solar is
       weakest (winter export only 937 kWh/yr) — so it is mostly grid-supplied.
     - Requires replacing the gas cooktop too before the connection can be cut;
       a partial disconnect keeps the $369 supply charge and destroys the case.
     - Capital cost of heat pump / induction not included. Check Victorian
       rebates (Solar Victoria, VEU) which may cover a large share.
     - If he moves to a cheap-overnight electricity plan, the heating runs even
       cheaper, improving this case further.
